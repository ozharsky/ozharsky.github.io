TestFlight

Help me break it.

Mossy is finished enough to live on a real phone and rough enough that you will find things. Both of those are the point.

Getting in

Two apps, about a minute.

Install TestFlight

Apple’s free beta app, from the App Store. Betas cannot be installed any other way.

Open the invite link

It opens TestFlight and adds Mossy to your list. Tap Install.

Scan something green

Point it at the nearest plant. That is the whole onboarding.

Open the TestFlight invite →

Requires an iPhone with Apple Intelligence.

What to try

The things most likely to be wrong.

Use it however you like — but if you want to be maximally useful, these are the parts with the least mileage on them.

Scan something obscure

Common houseplants work well. I want to know where it falls over — odd cultivars, seedlings, a plant with three species in one pot.

Check the toxicity line

If you know a plant is toxic to cats and Mossy disagrees, that is the single most important bug you can send me.

Argue with the chat

Tell it something the care report contradicts. It should believe you over a photo it took a week ago.

Add the widget and wait

Put it on the Home Screen, then leave the app closed for a few days. The day counts should keep moving on their own.

Tap a tag on a locked phone

If you have blank NFC tags, write one and tap it with the screen off. That path is the fiddliest in the whole app.

Use it for two weeks

The watering log only gets interesting once there is history in it. Most bugs in that area need time to show up.

Known rough edges

Things I already know about.

No point reporting these — but knowing them saves you from thinking the app is broken.

  • Pro is unlocked for testers. The paywall still appears so it can be tested, but the features behind it are open while you are on a TestFlight build. It is not a bug and you are not being charged.
  • Identification is only as good as the photo. Poor light and busy backgrounds drop the confidence hard. That is working as intended, but the guidance could be clearer.
  • Reference photos come from Wikipedia, so some species have none. You will see a placeholder rather than a wrong picture — that is deliberate.
  • Writing tags needs Pro and a real iPhone. There is no NFC in the Simulator.

Sending feedback

Screenshot, then shake.

In a TestFlight build, taking a screenshot offers to send it as feedback, and you can add a note. That goes straight to me with the device and build details attached, which saves a round of “which iPhone?”

If something is more of a conversation than a bug, email works too. Tell me what you expected to happen — that is usually the more useful half.

What Apple sends me

TestFlight shares crash logs and basic usage data, plus whatever feedback you choose to send. It never includes your plants or your photos — those never leave your phone. The privacy policy covers this in full.

Before you start

You need an iPhone with Apple Intelligence.

Identification runs on Apple’s on-device foundation model. That is what keeps your photos on your phone, and it is also a hard requirement — on a device without it, Mossy cannot identify anything and will tell you so rather than failing quietly.
