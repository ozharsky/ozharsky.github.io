The plant stakes

Four parts, one chip, no server.

A 25 mm NFC inlay sealed inside a printed green tag. Tap it and Mossy opens that plant. Tap it with anything else and you still get a page that tells you how to water it.

The set

Four shapes, because pots differ.

Every one takes the same 25 mm inlay and carries the same mark. Drawn here to a single scale.

01  Garden Stake

The outdoor one. A 110 mm shaft with a 24 mm point, so most of its length is below the soil and the head does not lean.

38 × 52 × 4 mm head

02  Indoor Stake

Shorter and thinner for houseplants. This replaced a pot-rim clip: rims run from 2 mm of nursery plastic to 20 mm of glazed ceramic, so no fixed clip gap fits a useful share of them. A spike fits every pot instead.

36 × 50 × 3.5 mm head

03  Soil Disc

Lies flat on the soil for pots too shallow to spike. Four scallops around the rim, because a flush disc is genuinely hard to get back out of wet soil.

∅34 × 3.4 mm

04  Hang Tag

A 4 mm hole for a hanging basket, a trellis, or a shelf hook. The only one that is wider than it is tall.

64 × 34 × 3 mm

Printing

The printer pauses, you drop the chip in.

There is no glue, no lid and no snap fit. The inlay is buried in the middle of the print.

The pocket floor lands at Z = 1.9 mm — 1.2 mm of cover between the readable face and the coil, plus a 0.7 mm pocket. Pause the print there, drop in the 25 mm inlay, resume, and the remaining layers close over it. The pocket is cut at 25.8 mm, which is 0.8 mm of clearance over a 25 mm inlay — about right for a 0.4 mm nozzle, though shrink varies by filament.

The mark is filament, not paint

The contactless glyph is built twice in the model: once as a 0.4 mm recess in the face, and once as a matching solid that fills it. Two separate bodies means a multi-material slicer can assign the mark its own colour with no painting and no post-processing. On a single-extruder printer, the recess alone still reads well.

It is there for a practical reason. NFC range is only 1–2 cm, so an unmarked face just gets waved at — and everyone already reads that symbol as “tap here” from payment terminals.

The paper slot is a trap, on purpose

A channel behind the face is 1.5 mm wider than the opening on every side, so a paper strip pushed in through the mouth at the right edge cannot fall out the front. Front skin 0.9 mm, channel 0.8 mm — paper or label stock both fit.

The chip

NTAG213 is enough. NTAG215 buys headroom.

Both are 25 mm round inlays and both drop into the same pocket. The only thing that changes is how much you can write.

Mossy checks the capacity before writing and drops optional parts of the message until it fits, so a 213 never fails for being small.

What gets written

One link, carrying the plant with it.

A Mossy tag holds a single URL. The plant’s id is the last path component, and the useful bits ride along in the query.

  • The id — what Mossy uses to open the right plant.
  • n — the plant’s name, clamped to 30 bytes.
  • w — watering interval in days.
  • l — light needs, clamped to 26 bytes.

Those extras exist for the people who are not you. Android taps a tag straight into a browser, and there is no Mossy for Android — so a tag carrying only an id would give them nothing. Carrying the name and care in the link makes the page self-describing with no server, no database and no account.

Why it all has to fit in one record

iOS and Android both act on the first NDEF record only. Anything in a trailing text record is invisible to them, so a second record would be dead weight. Everything worth reading goes in the URL.

Byte-counted, not character-counted — a “·” costs two.

The phone reads it

Nothing to open first. iPhone picks the stake up from the lock screen and offers the link.

Mossy opens that plant

It is a Universal Link, so it skips the browser entirely and lands on the plant’s own page.

Everyone else gets a page

No app, no problem — the link renders a care page built entirely from what the tag carries.

Writing a tag

Three taps, then hold it near the head.

Open the plant, choose Write to Plant Tag, and hold the top of your iPhone against the mark. Mossy writes the link, stamps the plant as tagged, and from then on that stake is that plant. Re-writing a tag just overwrites it.

Needs Mossy ProWriting tags is a Pro feature. Reading them is not — anyone can tap.

Needs a real iPhoneThe Simulator has no NFC hardware, and NFC needs a paid developer account to enable at all.
