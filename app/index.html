The app

Everything Mossy does.

One photo in, a full care report out — then the boring, useful part: remembering what you watered and when.

Identification

It tells you how sure it is.

Most plant apps hand you a name with total confidence and no way to argue. Mossy shows the number.

A scan runs Apple’s on-device foundation model over your photo, then cross-checks the answer against the Vision framework’s read of the plant’s form — whether it looks like a succulent, a fern, a vine, a palm. Agreement nudges the confidence up a little; disagreement nudges it down. It is capped hard in both directions, because Vision agreeing about shape is weak evidence about species and should never manufacture certainty.

Anything under 50% is flagged on the plant’s page with what to do about it: better light, get closer, aim at the leaves.

You get the final say

Every plant shows your photo next to a reference photo of the species. Confirm the match or correct the name — a correction sticks, and a re-scan never overwrites it.

Health & care

What it needs, and what’s wrong.

Each scan produces a health verdict and a full care sheet, written for the species it just identified.

  • Health — healthy, needs attention, or critical, with notes on what it actually saw.
  • Light, water, soil, temperature, humidity — in plain sentences, not icons you have to decode.
  • Pet safety — and where the species appears in a curated database derived from the ASPCA’s toxic plant list, that verdict replaces the model’s guess outright.
  • Growth habit and general tips for the long run.

A health read has a shelf life

It describes the photo you took, on the day you took it. Mossy treats it that way everywhere — including in chat, which will not tell you a plant looks healthy when you are standing in front of it saying otherwise.

Watering

The part you actually open it for.

Mossy reads the watering interval out of the care report — “soak every 7 days” becomes a number — and counts from the last time you logged one.

One tap to log

A Water button on the plant, on the journal card, and on the widget. The last thirty waterings are kept per plant.

30 waterings kept

Reminders at 9am

Opt in per plant. Notifications are one-shot, so Mossy re-arms them every time you open the app.

A widget that ages itself

Ask Mossy

A chat that has read the file.

Ask about the plant in front of you and the answer knows which plant that is.

The conversation is seeded with the species, the care report, the watering interval and how many days it has actually been. Ask “why are the tips brown?” on a plant nine days past its soak and the reply says so, rather than reciting a list of six possible causes.

It runs on the phone. No connection needed, nothing sent anywhere, and no per-message cost to anyone.

Requirements

What you need to run it.

  • DeviceiPhone with Apple Intelligence
  • IdentificationOn-device foundation model
  • Cross-checkVision framework
  • StorageOn device, no account
  • Writing tagsNFC-capable iPhone

Mossy leans entirely on Apple’s on-device model, which is what makes the privacy promise possible — and also what sets the hardware floor. Without Apple Intelligence available there is nothing to run the identification on, and the app says so plainly rather than failing quietly.

Reading a plant tag works on any modern iPhone. Writing one needs NFC and Mossy Pro.
